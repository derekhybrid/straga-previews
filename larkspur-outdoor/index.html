01

Walk the lot.

We mark the patio footprint with stakes and string, check the pitch away from the house and find the sightlines from your kitchen window.

Fall: ¼" per foot, away from foundation
02

Excavate.

A small excavator digs the footprint out ten inches below finished grade. Topsoil gets saved for the beds.

Depth: 10" · spoils hauled or reused
03

Build the base.

Six inches of crushed stone, compacted in two-inch lifts with a plate compactor. This is the part that decides whether the patio is still flat in fifteen years.

¾" DGA · compacted in 2" lifts
04

Screed the bedding.

One inch of concrete sand, pulled level with pipes and a straightedge so every paver sits the same.

Bedding: 1" concrete sand
05

Lay the pavers.

Set from the house outward in the pattern you chose, cut clean at the edges and locked in with a concrete edge restraint.

Pattern: running bond · 90° to house
06

Sweep the joints.

Polymeric sand swept into the joints, misted and set. No weeds, no ants, no washing out.

Joint: polymeric sand, ⅛"–¼"
07

Seat wall and fire pit.

A curved seat wall on the far edge and a fire pit at the center of it, so the patio has a reason to be out there after dark.

Wall: 18" high, 12" cap · pit: 44" ID
08

Beds and plantings.

Foundation beds against the house and a privacy row along the back line, planted for Zone 7a so they come back every spring.

Zone 7a · mulch 3" hardwood
09

Lawn.

The rest gets final grade and sod, rolled and watered in. Green by the weekend.

Tall fescue sod · 2" topsoil
10

Lights on.

Low-voltage path lights along the patio edge and uplights in the trees. The yard you use at 9 pm, not just at noon.

12V LED · 2700K warm
